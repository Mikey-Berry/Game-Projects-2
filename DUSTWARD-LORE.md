# DUSTWARD — Lore Bible

*Living reference, revision 2. Draw on this when writing new features so the fiction stays
consistent. Update it when new lore ships or Mikey rules on an open point.*

**Canon legend:** `[C]` established in shipped code. `[★]` canon per Mikey — authoritative even
where not yet in code. `[★prov]` canon but provisional (e.g. a name flagged for a later pass).
`[?]` deliberately open / mystery, may be retconned — do not resolve, and keep dependent
dialogue thin so it stays safe to revise.

**Two-layer theology:** anything the Church teaches is written as **The Church Teaches** (their
caricature) vs **The Truth** (what's real). NPCs speak from one layer; writers must know which.
The gap between the two layers *is* the setting's engine.

**Author-canon:** some of what follows — §4 in particular — is written so writers know what they
are gesturing at. It is not for characters to say. Where a section is author-only it says so.

**Scope:** Dustward. Mikey cross-pollinates his own worlds deliberately, so shared names and
motifs with New World RPG are intended, not errors. Alagadda is a deliberate borrowing from the
SCP shared universe.

**One-line premise:** Humanity spent an age drinking from a sleeping cosmic god through the
science it called alchemy. It noticed. The sky opened, the world burned, and now — powered by
the wound that never closed — even commoners work miracles by rote, never knowing the tap they
draw from already ended the world once.

---

## 1. The pitch `[★]`

Post-Fracture waste. Kenshi's indifferent frontier, amplified toward the eldritch. Alchemy is a
real science of formulae, siphoning power from the **Eldest** — a sleeping thing that must never
be allowed to wake.

A medieval kingdom found a way to tap it, prospered for centuries, then tried to cut out the
middleman and reach the source directly. **The attempt worked.** That is the whole tragedy: the
calculations were correct, the rite succeeded, and the world we play in is the Golden Age's
project completed. Every commoner working a formula today is finishing what killed them, and
nobody knows it.

## 2. Before the Golden Age `[★]`

There were two civilisations. The first was older than the human species and lived where the sky
could not see it.

Millions of years before anything called itself a kingdom there was a civilisation beneath the
earth — intricate pathways cut deep, an entire world under the world. They were **the original
progenitors of humankind**: pale, blind, deformed by modern standards, unused to sun and surface.

They were not hiding from weather. **They knew what the sky was.** The sky is the cosmos, and the
cosmos is the scrutiny of the Old Ones — and only the deep concealed them from that. Every fear
the modern world has of the dark, they had of the open air, and they were right.

**Their religion.** Life centred on the worship of slumbering Old Ones. And **Mother —
Llammialith — abandoned by her kin on their cosmic journeys and left to rot in the earth** —
became their priest: the one who interceded, on their behalf, with the true deity they actually
feared. Ainzopha'ar. She did not rule them and they did not worship her; she was the intercessor,
which is a smaller and stranger role, and it is the role the Golden Age would later rebuild as
plumbing. Ancient tablets, manuscripts and temple depictions record this hierarchy. **The temple
art points upward** — and that fact, read by the wrong people much later, is what ends the world.

> THE IRONY TO KEEP IN VIEW. The oldest, wisest civilisation on this planet went underground
> *specifically to avoid the notice of the thing in the sky*. The Church now worships it, by
> name, in the open air, at noon.

**What happened to them `[?]`.** They ended. What ended them is not yet determined and is
deliberately open — but it matters, because the Last Scholar is one of them, and everything he
has done since is an attempt to steer humanity away from a demise like his own people's.
Whatever it was, he watched it happen, and he thinks it is happening again.

**Where the humans came from.** *Some of them went up.* A population left the deep, adapted to
the sun across an enormous span, and forgot entirely where it had come from — those are the
surface humans. Millions of years later the ones who stayed below were dying, barrenness
commonplace among their women, the old ways fading. So they came up and bred back into their own
descendants, who did not recognise them. The consequence is quiet and total: **every human alive
is partly them.** Nobody knows it. Pale, light-averse, deep-easy traits surfacing in isolated
towns are not local peculiarity — they are the old line coming back up.

**The Deep `[★]` `[C]`.** The cave systems are not caves. They are a **million-year-old ecology**
— the ruins and technology of the first civilisation, layered with the things they contacted. The
Golden Age dug into somebody else's city and built vaults in it, which is why every cave in the
world bottoms out in "a sealed Golden-Age bunker and what it was built to keep." They may have
been far more advanced than anything since — capable of traversing the cosmos, and of contact
with other grand races and other dimensions. **Alagadda** is one such place (§14).

## 3. The Kingdom, and the rite `[★]`

The second civilisation. Roughly medieval, until it wasn't.

**The finding.** Science was alchemy, and the kingdom's alchemists theorised a power beyond human
imagining. Expeditions went beneath the earth tracing the legends of the purebloods, in what
should have been a doomed quest. **They succeeded.** Llammialith was contacted and the secrets of
the cosmos were unveiled. She was not hostile and not evil. She was **sad** — abandoned by her own
kin, looking for a surrogate in the lesser beings gathered around her. She had been a priest to a
congregation once; she gave the new ones what she had given the old ones.

> MOTHER WAS THE ONLY GREAT ONE THEY EVER FOUND. There was no second acquisition. **Malathuun
> was never located, never taken, never exploited** — it slept somewhere and woke during the
> Fracture, and was put down as a casualty of it. Anything in old notes describing the Golden Age
> as having "found two" is the Golden Age overstating how much of the world it controlled.

**The betrayal.** Having learned enough, the king's alchemists turned their power against her.
They did not move her — she was already in the earth. **They built the prison around a god who was
lying where she had always lain**, and began to siphon. The kingdom became the de-facto
sovereignty of its age and a golden age began, running for centuries on **her pain**, supplemented
by blood magic and the sacrifices of dark rites. A rite of that era could cost a hundred slaves.
In this period the three immortalities were found.

**Three words for one prisoner `[★]`.** Use the vocabulary by speaker; it encodes who is talking.

- **Priest** — what the purebloods called her, and what she actually was. An intercessor.
- **Conduit** — what the Golden Age's alchemists wrote in their notes. A component.
- **Battery** — the flat modern reading. Nobody in the world says this word; it is for us.

**The rite.** The Last Scholar re-appeared in this period, warned them, and was spurned; he then
quietly began building the thing that would save them (§11). Meanwhile the alchemists turned to
the temple depictions — the ones that **pointed upward**. If this much was possible with Mother as
a mere conduit, what might be possible against the source itself? Man challenged God. The rite
**succeeded**, and a path opened to Ainzopha'ar's throne.

> WHAT THE FRACTURE ACTUALLY WAS. **He did not wake.** He was disturbed, and he shifted — the
> equivalent of rolling over in sleep. That alone annihilated the kingdom at the centre in
> brilliant light, tore the fabric of reality, and opened a path between the material and the
> immaterial that has never closed. Nobody knows what would happen if he truly woke. Nobody has
> any intention of finding out. This is why the Messengers exist (§5).

**What it cost, and what it bought.** The alchemists' calculations were correct. They no longer
needed Mother to intercede; alchemy could be channelled directly, by anyone, from the wound.
**That is the world the game is set in.** It is the Golden Age's project, delivered, working
exactly as designed.

What they did not anticipate was everything else. The surge brought the **Watchers**, who unfolded
themselves from the angles of every fortress and fortification and killed everything they found.
And Mother, still in her cell, watched her own parasites — cut from her, with her permission, by a
man who told her why — used to put down Malathuun, who had woken into the same catastrophe. The
scholars who had foreseen it were among the few who lived. The Hollows saved what was left. The
Last Scholar guided the rebuilding.

**The tap, before and after `[★]`**

| | Before the Fracture | After |
|---|---|---|
| Route | Through Mother. She interceded; no other way through. | Straight out of the wound. No intermediary. |
| Cost | Metered and enormous. A rite could cost a hundred slaves. | Nearly free. Follow the formula and it works. |
| Who could | Whoever held her. One kingdom, impossibly far ahead. | Anyone. *Fallout, not birthright.* |
| Her status | The most valuable asset in the world. | **Redundant.** Still charged, still caged, and nobody came back. |

## 4. What is on the throne — AUTHOR-CANON `[★]`

*No character states this. It reaches the player, if at all, only peripherally and vaguely — a
Codex fragment, a Redoubt record, the Last Scholar being unhelpful.*

What the rite opened a path to was not an intelligent being to be bargained with, as Mother had
been. It was something **far older, and far simpler**.

**Ainzopha'ar is a blind idiot god.** Mindless. Not cruel, not watchful, not interested — the
source of all alchemy and possessed of no opinion whatsoever about anyone drawing on it. The
Church's entire doctrine of a searching gaze and a merciful hand is painted over something that
has never once perceived them. He is chained and bound by **celestial Messengers**, and barely
lulled to slumber.

> HE IS NOT A PRISONER. HE IS A PATIENT. He is not bound because he lost, or because he is weak,
> or because anything defeated him. **He must be kept asleep, or existence as everything
> understands it may end.** The Messengers are not jailers of an enemy. They are custodians of a
> catastrophe that has to stay unconscious. He rolled over in his sleep once and it took the sky
> off the world.

**On the blindness and the Eye.** Both are metaphor. He is an unspeakable and incomprehensible
thing and the words are the best a human record can do — "blind" for something that does not
perceive, "the Eye opened" for a stirring a survivor could only describe as being looked at. Do
not build mechanics on either image and do not let a character explain them.

## 5. The ladder of outsiders `[★]`

Classification is by nature, not chronology — time works differently for them, so *when* one came
to be is a non-factor.

- **The Eldest** — the sleeping apex and the source every gift draws on. **Mindless. Indifferent,
  not malicious** — the indifference is not a temperament, it is an absence. Held asleep. See §4.
- **The Messengers** — celestial. Not *above* the Eldest in power, but above everything in
  **function**: they are what keeps him under. Of an entirely different stock from the gaunts.
- **Old Ones** — the highest echelon of the rest. Named, world-shaping. "Great One" is a synonym.
- **Watchers** — the lower outsiders. They never noticed humanity until we stole power.
- **Gaunts** — folk-term for the lowest Watchers, the scavenger night-things in play. `[C]`
- **"Things Beyond"** — the umbrella folk and scholar term for all of the above.

**The Messengers `[★]`.** Intelligent Watchers in the loose sense that they are eldritch, but a
different stock entirely, and they should never be written as a gaunt variant. They speak an
ancient tongue nobody remembers or understands. They come in varieties — alchemist and warrior —
and use no technology at all, wielding weapons composed of fire and light out of their own latent
power. Those weapons are not lootable; they are not carried. They are **the ones who bound the
Eldest**, and they are still doing that job. Late in the world's clock, some walk among the
Paladins.

> WHY THE CHURCH CAN ALLY WITH THEM, AND BE HALF-RIGHT. If the Watchers are an interdimensional
> constabulary — and for authoring purposes they are — then a faith organised around suppressing
> profane alchemy is **pointed in a defensible direction for indefensible reasons**. The Purge's
> theology is wrong at every level. But "people who tap the source should be stopped" is a
> position the Messengers would recognise. A Paladin who fights beside one is not a traitor and
> not deceived. He is a man who reached a correct conclusion from a false premise and will never
> learn which half was which.

> MECHANICAL NOTE `[C]`. `hostile()` reads `if(a.faction === 'gaunt' || b.faction === 'gaunt')
> return true;` with the comment "the gaunts did not come here to take sides." That is correct and
> should stay. **Messengers therefore need their own faction, not `gaunt`.** Ordinary Watchers stay
> unconditionally hostile to everyone; Messengers get a faction that can stand beside `purge`.

> THE CONSEQUENCE, FOR WHOEVER WRITES THEIR DIALOGUE. The Watchers are not villains. They arrived
> when a species disturbed a bound god and have been killing everything near the breach ever
> since. **Closing the Door is not a defeat you hand them. It is a job you do for them.** Nobody
> in the world understands this, and the Messengers are not going to explain it in a language
> anyone can hear.

## 6. The pantheon

**Ainzopha'ar — the Eldest, and the Church's God**

- *The Church Teaches:* a metaphysical presence of all-consuming, infinite light (the name a
  corruption of *Ain Soph Aur*). His gaze wanders, searching for the lost sun, one day to return
  and scorch to light what heretics work in the dark. He is the source of miracles, extended as
  mercy to the penitent. Adherents must reconcile that he also brought the **Original Purge** —
  the Fracture — to punish humanity's impudence.
- *The Truth:* he is a **blind idiot god**, mindless and asleep, held under by the Messengers
  because the alternative may be the end of everything. He has no gaze, no mercy and no opinion.
  He did not punish anyone. He turned over once, because somebody knocked.

The Church prays to a thing that cannot hear, will never answer, and whose one recorded response
to being addressed was to annihilate the people who addressed it. **Keep this central whenever the
divine or the Purge appears.** The Church burns people for the profane gift and lights their pyres
with the blessed one `[C]`; a rift seals to blessed or cursed alike, because they draw on the same
tap `[C]`.

**Mother — Llammialith `[★]`, name `[★prov]`.** An Old One abandoned in the earth by her kin, who
became the purebloods' priest and then the kingdom's conduit. Sympathetic, sad, never hostile to
anyone. Still in a cell at the bottom of a sealed redoubt `[C]`. **Llammialith is her true name** —
a compound of Lamia and Lilith — known only to the precious few she has chosen to whisper it to.
The Golden Age never used it; they indexed her under a serial number. She is the origin of two
peoples: her spirit became the **Hollow**, her blood and tissue the **chimera** (§9). She permitted
the first and did not permit the second.

> THE THING SHE KNOWS. She let a man cut pieces off her to save a species that had already
> imprisoned her. Those pieces then killed **Malathuun**, another Old One, who had done nothing and
> woken into someone else's catastrophe. She watched it. She has had nine generations in a locked
> room to think about it, and she is the only being alive who knows the whole of what happened.
> This is the scene at the bottom of that cave, and nothing else in the setting carries more.

**Malathuun, the Sundered `[★]` `[C]`.** **Never found, never taken, never exploited.** An Old One
that slept somewhere and woke during the Fracture, put down by the Hollows as a casualty of a
disaster it had no part in. Dead, but never truly gone. Its body was scattered; its pieces are the
richest alchemical reagents in the world, so people hunt its corpse across the waste. Its
consciousness pervades everything, diffuse and lingering. A god turned into loot and into soil.
Where its fragments fell the deep waste breeds entirely new ecosystems `[C]`; the ground there
"breeds things that did not exist last year," and the fragments are hunted anyway — "the dead god
does not appear to mind. Appear."

**Old Har-Mageddon — the devourer `[★]`.** Devourer of worlds, seeker of endless war; an ouroboros
waiting to consume all at the end of time. In Paladin myth the dark contrast to Good Kami — some
read him as a metaphor for human greed, others as a genuine deity at war with Kami. Paladin lore
calls him the **sixth-born**; the cults call him the **seventh living** god. His place in the
revised cosmology is open `[?]`.

**Good Kami — the answered one `[★]`.** A legitimate Old One, sympathetic in spirit, and rare in
being benevolent at all. Folk-worship centres on him where official worship centres on Ainzopha'ar
— a presence felt in the dusty breeze and the dirty water, who **answers favourably when the rites
are observed to its liking**. Observed as law, not as creed. Never within view of the Church. **And
the warning:** vengeful curses invoked in its name are to be avoided at all cost. A benevolent god
that answers is still a god that answers, and what it does on your behalf when you ask it to hurt
someone is not a thing you get to take back. Candidate placement, unresolved `[?]`: Mother's kin
left her and went on cosmic journeys — Kami may be the one who stayed.

**Tohu & Bohu — the Voidborn Twins `[★]`.** Formlessness and Emptiness, born from the post-Fracture
vacuum — the death of the world and countless Old Ones left a feedback loop, and from it a gestalt
sentience arose. Temporary by nature, persisting only through a loophole: **while the Last Scholar
lives, the Twins are held in stasis.** He wrote that law with a Philosopher Stone and suspects it
was a mistake.

## 7. Alchemy: the Four Arts `[C]`

All "magic" is alchemy — formulae powered by the Eldest, now fuelled by Fracture residue. Every
branch is a degraded remnant of a greater Golden Age art. Commoners call it magic and follow it as
ritual; the educated know it as science.

- **Divine** — light, beauty, grandeur, healing, revelation. The Church's "miracles." **Revered.**
  Delve too deep and you are blinded by eldritch light and maddened by first-creation secrets.
- **Destruction** — altering matter and self toward the elements. **Feared as "wild magic."** A
  degraded remnant of golden-age **transmutation**, which once turned lead to gold and altered the
  states of matter itself.
- **Dark** — necromancy; a balance of the other paths. **Profane, feared, burned.** Undeath is a
  stolen parlour trick of the Old Ones.
- **Dust `[★]`** — the fourth branch and the world's namesake. Now mostly illusion, invisibility,
  alteration — a degradation of an art that *altered reality itself*. The true potential is lost to
  time and, fittingly, to dust. Practitioners: **dusters** `[★prov]` — itinerant, low-status,
  working under charlatan cover, which is perfect concealment because a real one can vanish, plant
  a false memory, or raise a wall that was never there.

**Attunement `[C]`.** A gift is **not permission** — it is a head start. Tier 0–3 in each branch,
earned by practice. A birth gift starts that branch at II; others start at 0 and can be trained.
**Only your birth branch reaches III.** Teachers accelerate, never gatekeep. A giftless unit who
reaches adept may claim a specialty. **Breadth costs** — each additional branch raises the
Attention per cast, which is the composition brake. Chimeras are alchemy-deaf with one exception
(§9); the Nascent Rite burns all attunement to nothing.

## 8. The three immortalities `[C]`

Discovered in the Golden Age, down each *main* branch. **Dust has no deathless path.** Its deep
tier stayed lost — the art that once altered reality is the one that cannot save you from anything.

> THE ROADS ARE OLD; THE WALKERS ARE NOT `[★]`. The three methods were found before the Fracture.
> **The people currently walking them ascended whenever they ascended.** Nothing new is invented in
> this world; it is only found again — which is exactly how the player acquires all three, out of
> recovered formulae.

| Road | Branch | Cost | What it leaves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lichdom** (The Last Rite) | Dark | A life, given whole | Transference favouring the eternal vigour of bone over decaying metal, with a phylactery for clean transfer across bodies and lifetimes. Ceiling 150. |
| **The Unclouding** | Divine | Toughness halved, forever | Endless healing and age-reversal, at the cost of a slow perfecting into an Old-One-like thing. Softer, not stronger. Grants Revive. Will not touch rot. |
| **The Sigil Rite** | Transmutation | The body; it will want ore, not bread | Transference into a vessel, usually armour. Preserves life and memory but costs alchemical ability. A crude transfer leaves shreds of consciousness piloting a decaying object. |

> THE HOLLOW LADDER IS NOT A FOURTH ROAD `[★]`. Awakening and the Nascent Rite are a **racial
> ascension**. The rite gates on having *any* gift to burn, not on Dust; a duster who takes it is
> coincidence, not requirement. Whether the ladder should eventually be reframed is open `[?]`.

**Lichdom in play `[C]`.** The one immortality the player wields. No crumble-on-death. A dying lich
sheds a **phylactery** where it fell; carry it to a Binding Circle and spend heavily to rebuild.
Leave it, and they wait in the dark. Liches keep enough of themselves that the Scholars ask, "Do
you still dream?"

**The bound are bound `[C]`.** Minded undead have convictions and can come to hate their master,
and cannot leave. Strain rises, convictions fire, the panel shows it — only the exit is shut. The
living can still hand in their notice, and that asymmetry is the moral texture of playing a
necromancer.

## 9. The races — *all player-recruitable*

> TWO PEOPLES OUT OF ONE PRISONER `[★]`. The Hollow got her **spirit**; the chimera got her
> **flesh**. They are the two highest ceilings in the game outside a lich, and that is not a
> coincidence. The difference between them is consent: **she permitted the Hollows.** Nobody asked
> her about the chimeras.

- **Human `[C]`** — "the stubborn default. Learns, breeds, dies on schedule." The one people nobody
  manufactured, and unknowingly the descendants of the deep. Seven lines, one per town.
- **Chimera `[★]`** — vat-grown war-stock for the Fracture war that was lost, **grown from
  Llammialith's blood and tissue**, reformed by rite into what was meant to be an army of perfect
  soldiers. Their defining wound follows: **alchemy-deaf, not untrained but incapable**, because a
  chimera is made of the medium the current runs through. Nobody crippled them; it could not have
  gone otherwise. The 140 ceiling is what a god's tissue is worth. Sterile — a line that ends with
  them.
- **Scaleborn `[★]`** — a chimera *line*, not a race. "Dragon-blooded," and **the only line that
  kept the art it was made with** — because Llammialith is Lamia as much as Lilith, and Lamia is a
  serpent. Every other chimera line is a failed attempt to make a soldier out of a god; Scaleborn
  is where they accidentally made a small piece of the god instead.
- **Hollow `[★]`** — the Last Scholar's Fracture weapon. He cut fragments from a captive god — **and
  she allowed it**, the nearest thing an Old One can manage to handing over her own body — and
  fitted them into trained eldritch hunters. Exposed to the fragments' full potential they lost
  memory and humanity, and *that loss is why they survived*: nothing of the mind remained to be
  broken. **Humanity survived the Fracture because of them.** Today the fragments run loose and
  infection is common; the Church derides them as "sick," pitiful.
- **Homunculus `[C]`** — vat-grown, learns frighteningly fast, dies at 34, sterile. Six lines poured
  for one job each. As empty vessels they attract **artificial souls** — stray alchemical overflow,
  an Old One's dream, even a whole Watcher. The Golden Age believed it was manufacturing labour.
- **Golem `[C]`** — "a servant that was left running until it began to want things." Clay, Rock,
  Paper, Glass.

**Hollow ascension `[C]` `[★]`.** *Awakened* costs thirty percent of every skill at once; *Nascent
Old One* costs the gift itself plus a Preserved Formula, and death becomes a formality — they
reform at the Binding Circle, gear left where they fell. The shipped comment says what this is:
"the riders are pieces of her. Ascension is a piece growing back toward the whole." Which means the
race blurb's "it is saving up for something" has an answer, and the answer is **her**. Every Hollow
is a small, distributed, extremely patient attempt by a prisoner to reassemble herself.

**Open, and on the shelf `[?]`:** exactly how the parasites work — biology, propagation, what a
fragment *is* — is undefined and wants a pass of its own.

**A line not yet written `[★]`.** Since some purebloods went up and bred back in, a
**pureblood-descended human line** is available and would slot into the existing seven without a
new system: pale, light-averse, easy underground, uneasy under open sky. **Saltmere** is the
ready-made home — it already "marries among themselves, buries among themselves, and counts every
stranger twice." Hollowmere's Grave-bred are the other candidate.

## 10. The Attention, the night & the deep `[C]`

**The Attention.** Necromancy draws the Watchers' notice through the leak. Four tiers, each
announced, and others can see the mark on you. **Unnoticed** → **Watched** ("Something out past the
fences has stopped moving. It is facing this way.") → **Attended** ("The dark has taken an interest
in you.") → **Seen** ("You are SEEN. Whatever is out there knows your name, and it is no longer
waiting.")

**Night ecology.** Gaunts are lower Watchers leaking through — **not native fauna** — and they take
no sides against anyone. Ordinary ones are *nightborn*: the dark made them and the dawn unmakes
them. Rift-held ones outlast dawn.

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|
| Gaunt | The common night-thing. "...khhhh..." |
| Thin Stalker | Fast, evasive, hard to pin. |
| Carrion Maw | Drags the downed and the dead into the dark and eats them limb by limb. |
| Hollow-Faced Shrike | Quick and brittle. Its bark is *(a sound like scissors)*. |
| Choir-Kin | Heavy, and it speaks in *(the chord)* — one held note. |
| Larder-Kin | It does not want to fight you. It wants *one* of you, and it will wait all night. Keeps a **midden** in the deep waste. You find it by following what took someone. |
| Brood-of-the-Door | **The thing holding it open**, shoulders in this world and the rest on the far side. Never enters, never leaves the Door's radius, and while it stands the closing rite cannot finish. |
| The Sixfold | Six legs, jointed wrong. Born when a tear reaches full width — "the rift collapses behind it, done with its purpose. It does not go back. It goes OUT." |

**Rifts, the Door, and the clock.** A **tear** is a place where the world did not knit back
together. Leave one open and it widens; at the second stage it begins to **sing**. **The Door** is
the Second Fracture made local: one tear, far larger, that does not drift shut. The Second Fracture
arrives on a calendar — roughly a hundred and eighty days, whatever anyone does. Living loudly
advances it; the floor is fixed. Six stages: **The Dust Falls**, **A Stillness**, **The Thinning**,
**The Watchers Wake**, **The Sky Leans**, **The Second Fracture**.

**A Stillness is the hinge.** It has happened twice — once nine generations ago, and once eleven
seconds ago with the player standing in it. The first Fracture did not close itself. Somebody shut
it, by hand, standing in it, holding a formula until it gave, and the working was written down.

**Sundered ground.** Where a piece of Malathuun fell — ribs like a burned cathedral, a skull you
could camp inside, a hand still reaching. Harvested for **Sundered Marrow**. The canonical well for
future creatures and biomes.

**The Cairn Beast.** Forms where the dead already are, and every number in its stat line derives
from how much it has eaten. A new one rises for every twenty-six unclaimed bodies left lying about.
The player's own housekeeping is the difficulty dial.

**Lunar events `[★]`.** Several exist; the **blood moon** is one `[C]`. The moon that used to
explain Scaleborn births is now unattached and deliberately kept — see §18.

## 11. The Last Scholar & the Scholars

**The Last Scholar `[★]`.** The First Scholar and the Last Scholar are one person; the titles are
two eras of the same life. **He is a pureblood** — one of the first civilisation, older than any
record humanity keeps. He appears human. Those who meet him report immense age, wisdom, and
**melancholy — a voice tinged with regret**. He was *the First Scholar* when he brought alchemy to
humanity, and he is *the Last Scholar* because he is the only one of his kind left; the modern
Scholars took their name from the second title without knowing there had been a first. **He appears
and reappears throughout history** — exactly the shape of the alchemical legends of the real world,
and meant to be.

> WHAT HE IS TRYING TO PREVENT `[?]`. His own people ended. **What ended them is not determined**,
> and it is the single most load-bearing open question in the setting — because everything he has
> ever done is an attempt to steer humanity away from a demise like theirs, and he believes it is
> happening again. He gave humanity alchemy. He came back mid-Golden-Age to tell them to stop. They
> spurned him. **Warning them was not prophecy. It was guilt.**

What he did:

- **Engineered the Hollows.** He cut fragments from Llammialith — with her permission, having told
  her what they were for — and fitted them into trained hunters. Humanity survived because of it.
  He is not a hero and he is not a villain, and should never be written as either.
- **Holds the Voidborn Twins in stasis.** Fearing the void-manifest would consume itself and take
  humanity's last remnant with it, he invoked a Philosopher Stone to write a new universal law:
  *while the Last Scholar lives, the Twins exist in stasis.* He suspects it was a mistake. His
  continued existence is load-bearing for the world.

> HIS IMMORTALITY IS A MYSTERY, DELIBERATELY `[?]`. He is on none of the three roads. His people
> were long-lived but not deathless, and millions of years for a civilisation is not millions of
> years for a man. **Do not explain it.** The legends this character is built on work precisely
> because nobody ever produces the mechanism.

**The Scholars `[C]`.** Wandering alchemist-cartographers dedicated to **knowledge over reverence**,
who understand themselves as having picked up the mantle — or the shadow of one — of the last
Golden-Age scholar. They are wrong about which age he came from and right about everything else.
They call it alchemy, not magic, and they are correct. The only people who treat a necromancer as a
colleague. They keep ledgers of which corpse-fields have gone quiet, which is how you find Lyre.
**Formulae** are the physical remnants of Golden-Age knowledge and the only source of insight:
*nothing new is invented in this world; it is only found again.* Voice: dry, precise,
gallows-humoured, professionally curious.

> "Every alchemist I ever admired is dead, mad, or has stopped aging. There is no fourth outcome."

## 12. Factions & the two religions

**The Church / the Purge `[C]` `[★]`.** Worships Ainzopha'ar. Burns the profane gift while lighting
pyres with the blessed one. The truth that all four branches share one source is known or suspected
by only one or two leaders and closely guarded; **most genuinely believe.** Paladins, escalating
wrath, daily hunts, and inquisition visits to towns that tolerate you. They hold the Bastion. The
Church existed *before* the Fracture — the Transmutation Codex is a pre-Fracture Church text —
which means it stood alongside the kingdom while the alchemists worked. What it knew, and whether
it blessed the rite, is open `[?]`, and is where the closely-guarded secret came from. Late in the
clock, **Messengers walk among the Paladins** (§5).

**Folk religion `[★]`.** The lived faith under the official one. Old Ones like **Good Kami** answer
*ritual*, not creed: dirty water at a crossroads, dust not swept past a threshold at night, a dead
name spoken into the wind. The Church calls it superstition; the Church is wrong, and quietly
everyone still does it. Fallowend is where it is thickest — "every lintel hung with old charms," and
its great house is the CHARM HOUSE `[C]`. The curse clause is why the practice survives as strict,
fussy, unexplained rule-following rather than devotion: **you do not improvise with something that
says yes.**

**The Coil `[C]`.** The Har-Mageddon doom cult. From the shipped comment: *"The seventh living god
has quiet people in every town. They pay their tithes, sweep their steps, and after dark some of
them walk out past the fences to a stone that is not on any map, and practice. Not worship.
Practice."* Five ordinary civilians marked at worldgen — never at Hollowmere — with an exposure
state and two sets of dialogue depending on it.

**The Compact `[C]`.** The player's institution, deliberately expensive. A town that signs puts
stores into a common stock and its guards into a shared muster — so **a signatory is immediately
weaker for it**, and only stronger if enough others sign. Three seats makes it a Compact. Conquest
is the worse bargain: a town held at knifepoint contributes half what a willing one does and
resents the levy twice as hard.

**The Redoubts `[C]`.** *"Some scholars saw it coming. They built bunkers, grew soldiers, forged
weapons the like of which nobody has managed since — and it made no difference whatsoever. Nine
generations on, the doors are still shut and the garrison is still at its post."* Warden Automatons
and crazed homunculi holding a line that ended nine generations ago: *"THE LINE HOLDS." "I was made
for this day. The day never came." "Are you the relief? You are nine generations late."* They were
dug into the first civilisation's tunnels, and at the bottom of one of them is Mother.

**Wardens, bandits, slavers, warbands, drifters, wild fauna `[C]`** — town defence and road hazards.

## 13. Named figures `[C]`

- **Verity, the Unclouded** (f) — see below. Gates The Unclouding.
- **The Sigil-Bound** — transmutation immortal in an armour vessel, one of eleven poured, two
  hundred years ago. "Nobody has seen it open" — opening it ends it. He is losing his name and the
  questline is him getting it back. Sex unfixed `[?]`.
- **The Ossuary King** (m) — dead royalty holding court in a far ruin, who refused to finish. He was
  at the pouring and signed for it — eleven names and his hand under all of them. Wears the Sunken
  Crown. See the note below.
- **Sister Ash** (f) — exiled inquisitor with a pyre-weapon. The light gave her certainty where it
  gave Verity quiet. "I burned my order. Bones burn easier."
- **Grand Marshal Vey** — holds the Bastion.
- **The Red Baroness** (f) — bandit warlord. The one antagonist with no cosmic dimension at all,
  which is why she works.
- **The Grazer** — a leviathan that wants nothing from anybody.
- **Lyre d'Alagadda** (f) — sent through before her brother, for trying to stop what he was willing
  to do. Eleven years closing tears by hand — the one thing nobody does. **The only character doing
  the opposite of what the player does, without being an enemy for it.**
- **Osric & Wenna** — Hollowmere gravekeepers who compete with you for corpses.
- **The Last Scholar `[★]`** — §11. Not yet in the code, and the largest unbuilt figure in the setting.
- **Mother · Llammialith `[★]`** — §6. Alive, redundant, and the only being who knows the whole of it.

**Verity, rewritten `[★]`.** **She ascended after the Fracture, not before** — ninety years ago,
four generations back, not an ancient. She was a Scholar, and her tragedy is that **she succeeded.**
She believed she had found the best of the three roads: unlike the lich she kept her body, unlike
the Sigil-Bound she could still eat, still touch, still feel. It looked like the version with no
price on it. The price arrived slowly. What the Unclouding took was not the senses but the
*emotional* capacity behind them — and it took it gradually enough that she watched it go. She is
what is left: a woman who can describe exactly what she used to feel and cannot produce any of it.
The shipped bark is already the thesis: *"I have not eaten in ninety years. I do not miss it. That
is the frightening part."*

She is the setting's clearest statement of its own genre: the scholar who pursued a thing, obtained
it exactly as specified, and was hollowed out by the getting. Nothing lied to her. Nothing tricked
her. **It worked.** Her five requests all read as experiments run by a researcher on her own
condition, and the last is her asking a stranger to stand there while she tries, and fails, to want
to stop.

> ON THE OSSUARY KING `[★prov]`. That he is the Golden Age's last king — the hand under all eleven
> names, holding court in the ruin of the kingdom itself — is **a rumour among scholars and
> unconfirmed knowledge, not established fact.** Write it as something the lettered pass around,
> never as something the world states. He is a legacy boss from the earliest editions and his
> current shape will not carry that weight; if he is ever reworked, the door is open.

## 14. Where the player comes in `[C]`

Five roles anybody could have had — **Gravekeeper**, **Exile**, **Freed Slave**, **Ruined
Merchant**, **Old Soldier** — and two people in particular:

- **Lyonart d'Alagadda** — exiled prince, **from another world**. Came through with the Sunken
  Crown, six of his household still following, and one sworn man still breathing. Begins holding the
  endgame and unable to reach it. His road is social — ask in halls, three tellings, rumour — and it
  ends at a sister who has spent eleven years undoing his kind of work.
- **Saga Wordsworth** — a Hollow, and not an accident: the Scholars grew godkillers in the redoubts
  and he walked out of one. Begins one rung up the ladder, the thirty percent already paid, and the
  next rung costs the art that makes him himself. His road is spatial and bodily. He is not looking
  for a god; he is looking for the rest of himself.

**Alagadda `[★]`.** An **interdimensional kingdom built on alchemy**, and one of the places the
first civilisation reached in its cosmic travels. The game's own vocabulary already carries this:
everything that arrives from beyond *comes through* — the gaunts came through, the Sixfold came
through, the Brood has its shoulders through — and Lyonart "came through at the edge of the world,"
Lyre "came through before you." Written as exile-flavoured phrasing, and literal after all.
**Exile there is one-way**, and Alagadda is not a place a person would go to. It is not a refuge and
not an escape route; for most it is a fate considerably worse than dying here. That is why nobody
leaves through the tears instead of closing the Door.

**Convictions `[C]`.** Everyone who follows you carries one and it decides what they forgive. The
**compassionate** hate a sacked town; the **devout** loathe lichdom above all; the **ambitious** want
conquest and resent retreat; the **cruel** enjoy the sack; the **inquisitive** care about recovered
formulae and would very much like to meet Mother; the **haunted** care about sealed tears and the
Door above everything. **Cold** mostly wants paying. Education is a damper, not a direction — the
lettered move slower in both directions.

## 15. Golden Age relics

- **The Aether Lance `[C]`** — transmutes latent ether to plasma. **Only the alchemically deaf can
  carry one** — in a gifted hand the interference cascades and takes both weapon and wielder. The
  Golden Age's best weapons can only be held by people it deliberately crippled to hold them.
- **Philosopher Stones `[★]`** — condensed alchemical might able to rewrite the laws of reality, one
  use, with all the contamination hazard that implies. Golden Age power sources for when latent
  aether was scarce. One wrote the law that holds the Twins.
- **Tablets of the Deep `[★]`, not yet built** — the purebloods' tablets, manuscripts and temple
  depictions. Unlike Formulae and Codices they would give **no insight at all**, only what happened.
  A collectible whose entire value is lore, found in the Deep, readable only by the lettered. The
  most promising route by which a player could assemble the cosmology without Saga's questline.
- **Codices & Formulae `[C]`** — the physical remnants of Golden-Age knowledge, and the only source
  of insight. The Transmutation Codex is "a pre-Fracture Church text, never burned because nobody
  dared. It names the source of the blessed gift and the profane one. It names them once, because
  they are the same name." The name is Ainzopha'ar.
- **Automatons `[C]`** — Golden-Age constructs; litter the Fracture-war ruins.

## 16. Places `[C]`

Open water is at the rim; the interior is continuous waste. The tree palette is dead husks
everywhere — **Greenrest is the sole oasis exception**, and that is why it is worth so many
watchmen. *Map dimensions are a development number and are deliberately not stated here.*

| Seat | In its own words |
|---|---|
| Dustport | A rough harbor town. Everything washes up here eventually — cargo, drifters, bodies. |
| Copperhold | Mine money built these walls, and mine money hires the blades on them. |
| Greenrest | Quiet fields, full granaries, and more watchmen than any town its size. They like it that way. |
| Hollowmere | No walls, no fear. The wardens' fire and the gravekeepers' dead are gate enough. The only seat where a necromancer is a professional rather than a problem, and the only one with a great structure — **THE HOLLOW CITADEL**, four floors. What it is for is open `[?]`. |
| Saltmere | A shuttered town on the salt flats. They marry among themselves, bury among themselves, and count every stranger twice. The best home for a pureblood-descended line. |
| Ironscar | An open pit worked by the indebted, order kept by hired blades over a workforce that would knife them for a day off. |
| Fallowend | The last farmstead before the map runs out. Every lintel hung with old charms. The folk religion's home ground. |

Beyond them: **the Bastion** (Paladin ground), **the redoubts** (sealed, garrisoned, dug into older
tunnels, and one holds her), **the Deep** (the first civilisation's world, layered under
everything), **the sundered ground** (Malathuun's corpse-fields), **the middens**, **the Coil's
stone** (on no map), and **the deep waste** — where the night comes from.

**Unbuilt and wanted `[?]`:** the kingdom's crater. It was annihilated at the centre in brilliant
light and should be the largest landmark in the world. It is also where the rite was performed,
which makes it the obvious place for the Door to open.

## 17. Aesthetic key & voice guide `[★]`

- **"Eldritch" is grotesque only through physical eyes.** To an ascended being the outsiders are
  beautiful — raw thought and power, true divinity. Horror and divinity are the same thing seen from
  different eyes; the Church isn't simply wrong to revere.
- **Commoners say "magic"; the educated say "alchemy."** Commoners say "gaunts"; the lettered say
  "Watchers." Word choice signals who a character is.
- **The gap is the story.** A religion built on a sleeping idiot god that ended everything, calling
  it mercy.
- **Nobody has the whole picture.** The Church has a caricature, the folk have a rumour, the Scholars
  have a dating error, and the player is the only one positioned to assemble it. *The player knows.
  That is the joke.*

1. Terse, physical, gallows-dry frontier plainspeak. No wizards, no chosen ones.
2. Show cost — everything good is paid for by someone bleeding.
3. Magic is labour and science, not wonder; practitioners are technicians and heretics.
4. Horror is ecological — hungry, patient, out past the light. Understatement over gore.
5. Understate the apocalypse. The dust is *not ash, not soil, not anything, and it is still falling*.
6. Let the specific do the work. Not "a long time ago" but *nine generations*. Not "briefly" but
   *eleven seconds*. Not "several" but *eleven names*.
7. End on the turn. *"They took that from me and called it a blessing." "He's better company now."
   "You are nine generations late."*
8. Names: plain-harsh for people, grim-title for powers, flat-descriptive for places.
9. **Never dump lore at the player.** It lives in barks, item text and stumbled-into lines. Deepen
   the question; never hand over the cosmology. One good line beats a paragraph.
10. **Know your layer.** A paladin speaks the Church's truth; a Scholar the real one; a commoner
    folk-rumour. Don't mix them in one mouth.

## 18. Open questions `[?]`

Flagged, unresolved, safe to revisit. Keep dependent dialogue thin.

| Question | Where it stands |
|---|---|
| What ended the first civilisation | **The most load-bearing open question in the setting.** Everything the Last Scholar does is an attempt to stop it happening to humanity, and he thinks it is happening again. |
| The Last Scholar's immortality | **Deliberately never explained.** The legends this character is built on work because nobody produces the mechanism. |
| How Mother's parasites work | Moral shape settled — cut from her, and she allowed it. The biology is undefined and is on the shelf for its own pass. |
| The Ossuary King as the last king | Scholar rumour and unconfirmed knowledge only. Legacy boss; the door reopens if he is reworked. |
| The kingdom's crater | Unbuilt. Should be the largest landmark in the world, and the natural site for the Door. |
| What the Church knew | It existed before the Fracture, alongside the kingdom. Whether it blessed the rite is unwritten, and it is where the closely-guarded secret came from. |
| Good Kami's placement | Candidate: the one of Mother's kin who did not leave. |
| Har-Mageddon's placement | No slot in the revised cosmology yet, and the Coil is already shipped. |
| God birth-order | First through seventh; five undefined; **do not resolve.** The shipped Coil comment says "seventh living god" and that is as far as anything should go. |
| The eldritch moon | Recycled and held. It used to explain Scaleborn births and cannot now. Candidate: the Voidborn Twins, who have no worldly signature at all. |
| The Hollow ladder | Not a fourth branch immortality. Whether it should be reframed or extended is for workshopping. |
| The Hollow Citadel | Exists, four floors, at Hollowmere. Purpose unwritten. |
| Mother's serial number | Needs a format. Make it clerical — an asset tag, not a codename. It should look like the requisition number on the Ordnance Schematic. |
| Sanctified Ash | Is a shrine stone a small Philosopher Stone, or something else the faithful mistook for one? |
| "Dusters" | Shipped; overlaps Dust / dust bandits / Dustport. Kept — the overlap reads as slang doing several jobs. |
| Broken decay | "The Fracture broke decay itself — the waste as preservative" is a canonisation candidate, nearly free against the existing rot ladder. |

**Forthcoming, and where it fits.**

- **Malathuun's Curse** — a sundered site stands up. Not a new rule: the corpse-sites *are*
  Malathuun, the ground there already breeds things that did not exist last year, and "the dead god
  does not appear to mind. Appear." has been waiting for something to justify that last word. It
  fits better now that he was a bystander rather than a resource: the one Old One in this story who
  was owed nothing and lost everything.
- **Eyes of Ainzopha'ar** — the Eldest's own eyes, and the first thing it has ever put into the
  world. Note the tension worth exploiting: he is mindless, so they cannot be *sent*. They would be
  something a sleeping thing sheds.
- **Messengers** — now the jailers (§5). Their tongue is the language the tap was designed in; you
  do not wire a sleeping god into a prisoner by accident. **They need their own faction in code, not
  `gaunt`.**
